# 7.3 teardown to bare block, 2026-10-21

What to look at, photograph, and keep while it comes apart. Everything the machine shop and the parts order need comes from today.

## Photograph before it leaves the stand

- [ ] Casting date on the block if you find one (clock-face or date wheel, usually on the side of the block or the bellhousing flange)
- [ ] Any stamped pad near the oil cooler mount on the passenger side rear. That is the serial if it is still there.
- [ ] Both heads, casting numbers and any date marks
- [ ] Crank snout and rear flange, stamped numbers
- [ ] Each rod, big end and beam, before they go in the box

## Rods: forged or PMR

Look at the cap parting line.
- **Forged:** smooth machined parting face, rounded beam, no fracture texture
- **PMR (powdered metal):** fracture-split cap, rough crystalline mating face, sharper beam edges

Doesn't change the plan (II forged rods going in) but write it down and photo it.

## Keep, bag, and label

The shop needs these in hand to bore and balance. Do not lose:
- [ ] Crank, with the damper and flexplate (balance assembly)
- [ ] All 8 rods with caps on, numbered, in cylinder order
- [ ] All 8 pistons, numbered, even though they are getting replaced (shop wants to see wear pattern)
- [ ] Main caps, in order, with bolts
- [ ] Cam and all 16 lifters
- [ ] Injector cups if they pull without destroying them (reference for the new brass ones)
- [ ] Oil cooler header (serial number pad, and you need it for the reassembly)
- [ ] Piston cooling jets, 8 of them. Inspect for bent or plugged.
- [ ] Rear main housing
- [ ] HPOP, reservoir, and the HPO rails even though the pump is being upgraded
- [ ] All injector hold-down bolts, rocker arms, pushrods (sort by cylinder)

Number everything with a paint marker before it comes out: cylinder number, cap orientation.

## Inspect and note

- [ ] Cylinder walls: scoring, ridge at the top, rust pitting from sitting. Photo the worst one. This decides .020 vs .040 vs sleeve.
- [ ] Crank journals: scoring, discoloration (blue = heat). Run a fingernail across each.
- [ ] Bearings: read them. Copper showing, embedded debris, or uneven wear tells you about the oil system history.
- [ ] Cam lobes and lifter faces: pitting or wiped lobes
- [ ] Head gaskets: any sign of pushing between cylinders or into coolant passages. Note which cylinders.
- [ ] Deck surface: erosion around coolant holes, fire ring impressions
- [ ] Valves: tuliped exhaust valves (the Dave's Auto video found this)
- [ ] Injector cups: cracks or coolant staining
- [ ] Block coolant passages: how much scale and rust is in there. Shop will bake and blast it but you want to know.
- [ ] Freeze plugs: any weeping
- [ ] Oil cooler core: debris in it tells you if something was coming apart
- [ ] Piston crowns: cracks between valve reliefs, melted edges, which cylinders
- [ ] Any broken bolts, stripped holes, or helicoils already in the block

## Measure if you have the tools handy

Shop will do all this properly, but a quick number now lets us order sooner:
- [ ] Bore at the top of ring travel vs bottom, on the worst-looking cylinder. Stock 4.11". If the ridge is under .005 and no pitting, .020 is likely enough.

## Tell me

Send photos and the answers to these and I log them:

4. Any surprises (cracked piston, spun bearing, bad cup)
